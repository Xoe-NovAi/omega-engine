**The 42 Ideals of Maat**  
*(also called the 42 Negative Confessions or Declarations of Purity)*

### Historical Core

In ancient Egyptian religion, Maat (or Ma’at) was both a goddess and the fundamental principle of truth, balance, order, harmony, law, morality, and justice. She was the opposite of chaos (Isfet).

When a person died, their heart was weighed against the feather of Maat in the Hall of Two Truths. Before that weighing, the deceased recited a series of negative confessions to 42 assessor gods (one for each nome of Egypt). These statements took the form:

> “I have not [committed this wrong].”

This is why they are called the **Negative Confessions**. The most famous version comes from the *Papyrus of Ani* (Book of the Dead, c. 1250 BCE), translated by E.A. Wallis Budge and others. There is no single canonical list that was identical in every papyrus; slight variations exist, but the moral content is consistent.

### Classic Negative Form (Papyrus of Ani tradition – abbreviated modern English)

1. I have not committed sin / iniquity.  
2. I have not committed robbery with violence.  
3. I have not stolen.  
4. I have not slain men or women.  
5. I have not stolen grain / food.  
6. I have not purloined offerings.  
7. I have not stolen the property of the gods.  
8. I have not uttered lies.  
9. I have not carried away food.  
10. I have not uttered curses.  
11. I have not committed adultery / fornication.  
12. I have made none to weep.  
13. I have not eaten the heart (i.e., not grieved uselessly or felt destructive remorse).  
14. I have not attacked any man.  
15. I am not a man of deceit.  
16. I have not stolen cultivated land.  
17. I have not been an eavesdropper.  
18. I have not slandered anyone.  
19. I have not been angry without just cause.  
20. I have not debauched the wife of any man.  
21. I have not polluted myself.  
22. I have not terrorized anyone.  
23. I have not transgressed the law.  
24. I have not been exclusively angry / hot-tempered.  
25. I have not closed my ears to the truth.  
26. I have not cursed the divine.  
27. I have not acted with violence.  
28. I have not stirred up strife.  
29. I have not acted hastily or without thought.  
30. I have not overstepped my boundaries.  
31. I have not exaggerated my words / been verbose in speech.  
32. I have not committed evil.  
33. I have not worked witchcraft against the king / authority.  
34. I have not stopped the flow of water.  
35. I have not raised my voice / been loud-voiced in anger.  
36. I have not cursed God.  
37. I have not acted with insolence.  
38. I have not been violent in my actions.  
39. I have not been impatient.  
40. I have not sought after distinctions / honors unduly.  
41. I have not increased my wealth except by my own just means.  
42. I have not scorned the god of my city / local divinity.

*(Exact wording varies slightly by translation and papyrus.)*

### Modern Positive Re-framings

In contemporary Kemetic, Isis, and spiritual communities (notably the work associated with Lady Loreon Vigne / Temple of Isis), the same principles are often rewritten in positive, affirmative form for daily living. One widely circulated modern list begins:

1. I honor virtue  
2. I benefit with gratitude  
3. I am peaceful  
4. I respect the property of others  
5. I affirm that all life is sacred  
…and continues through 42 positive statements of character and conduct.

These positive versions are modern compositions inspired by the ancient negative confessions; they are not direct ancient texts.

### Summary

The 42 Ideals of Maat are one of the oldest surviving comprehensive ethical codes in human history. They are not a list of commandments handed down from on high, but a set of declarations the individual soul makes about how it has chosen to live. The negative form is historically authentic; the positive form is a modern adaptation for practical daily use.
