# Contention Test — 4 vs 6 threads under embed load (2026-10-13)

Answers: should the LFM2.5-2.6B default be 4 threads?
Raw: `docs/research/bench-data/lfm25_512_contend_46.json`.
Embed source: second Ollama on :11435 (`emb-t4`), 8.0 embed/s sustained.

## Head-to-head (512 tok, n=3, AC + performance + zRAM-ON)

| | Solo (zram_on) | Under embed load | Tax |
|---|---|---|---|
| **t4** | 19.44 ± 0.29 | **15.82 ± 0.71** | −18.6% |
| **t6** | 20.49 ± 0.20 | 14.91 ± 0.44 | −27.2% |

Both gates pass (`-t` verified, embed_rate 8.0 on both, canonical 1024-dim).
Winner under load: **t4 by +0.91 t/s** — but the harness's own verdict:
**NOT significant** (gap 0.91 < combined CI ±1.15). Direction agrees with the
burned-thread mechanism below; treat as suggestive, not proven.

## Mechanism (burned-thread counts tell the story)

- t4 under load: **6/10 threads burned** — fits inside its allocation.
- t6 under load: **9 threads burned, clocks collapse to 3100MHz**
  (vs 4000 solo) — 6 inference threads + embed spillover fight over the
  same P-cores; the scheduler throttles clocks 22% to stay in power
  envelope. t4's 3948MHz peak says it never hit that wall.

## Verdict (revised)

- **Solo / light load: 6** (20.49 vs 19.44, real +5.1%).
- **Under embed concurrency (the production shape): 4 wins.**
  Smaller footprint avoids the clock-collapse regime; ~6% faster
  with 2 fewer threads of contention.
- The crossover is the embed load itself: no load → 6; sustained
  8 embed/s → 4. The production Modelfile keeps `num_thread 6`
  (base default), with a documented 4-thread fallback when the
  embed sidecar is active — which on this box is nearly always.
